Don Norman first published The Design of Everyday Things in 1988. The revised edition came out 25 years later, in 2013. I'm reading it again, for the 3rd or 4th time, in 2026.

In 1988, we had desktop computers and design thinking was new as a coherent discipline. In 2013, we had a lot of "web 2.0" tools available, and Apple had popularized design as a powerful model for human-centered technological change. They had proven that design could and should go hand-in-hand with business. In 2026, we're in the age of AI.

Norman's approach to design stands on its own, and will outlive the phases of social and technological change the book has been through already. Fundamentally, he is just asking us to be deeply empathetic, to know what people need and want, to address those needs and wants in our designs, and to pay attention to all the ways that objects, interfaces, or "things" fit into a human lifeworld.

In the revised 2013 edition, he added two chapters: one on "Design Thinking" as a model, and the second on "Design in the World of Business" where he predicts "the rise of the small":
> I dream of the power of individuals, whether alone or in small groups, to unleash their creative spirits, their imagination, and their talents to develop a wide range of innovation. New technologies promise to make this possible. Now, for the first time in history, individuals can share their ideas, their thoughts and dreams. They can produce their own products, their own services, and make these available to anyone in the world. All can be their own master, exercising whatever special talents and interests they may have.
> 
> What drives this dream? The rise of small, efficient tools that empower individuals. The list is large and growing continuously. Consider the rise of musical explorations... of self-publishing... billions of small videos... universities sharing whole curricula... the same phenomena in writing, reporting events, and teh creation of music and art... consider the rise of specialists to help plan meals and cook them, to modify designs to fit needs and circumstances, to tutor on a wide variety of topics...
> 
> I dream of a renaissance of talent, where people are empowered to create, to use their skills and talents.
> 
> ...the world is rapidly changing. Design is a powerful equalizing tool: all that is needed is observation, creativity, and hard work -- anyone can do it.
> 
> We can transform the world.

Norman is obviously an optimist -- he believes in world transformation, in a global civil discourse that drives progress through empowerment, in openness and the "creative spirit". He would marvel at the ways that AI expands this dream.
